Stand on the road below Baños and listen. The Pastaza river is already loud, and somewhere ahead, hidden by wet green walls of forest, it drops through the Pailón del Diablo with a roar you feel in your chest before you see the water. Behind you are the high, bare slopes of the Andes. Ahead, the valley keeps falling towards the Amazon. In one afternoon's drive the air goes from thin and cool to thick and warm, and that is the whole idea of Ecuador in miniature.

The country's tourism ministry calls it ["one destination, four worlds"](https://ecuador.travel/): the Andes, the coast, the Amazon and the Galápagos. It sounds like a slogan, and it is one, but it is also simply true. Few countries let you change climate, landscape, wildlife and food this completely without a long-haul flight in between. Here is what each world is like to be in, what to see there, how they join up, and, for the coast, what current advice means for your plans.

## The Andes: volcanoes, markets and thin air

The Andes are the spine of Ecuador and, for most visitors, the first world they meet. Quito, which Ecuador Travel describes as [the oldest and highest capital in Latin America](https://ecuador.travel/andes/), sits in a long valley under green mountains. Its old town is all whitewashed walls, carved church doors and plazas where the light is sharp and the shade is cold. You walk slowly, partly out of respect for the altitude and partly because there is so much to stop and look at. Our [Quito in three days](/posts/quito-in-three-days/) covers it properly.

South of the capital runs what Ecuador Travel calls the [Avenue of the Volcanoes](https://ecuador.travel/avenida-de-los-volcanes-de-ecuador/), where more than 70 volcanoes and mountains line the route. The one you'll see on postcards is Cotopaxi, a near-perfect snow cone at 5,897 m that the [Instituto Geofísico](https://www.igepn.edu.ec/cotopaxi) lists as active and which lies about 45 km south-east of Quito. Further south, Chimborazo rises to 6,263 m, according to the [Instituto Geofísico](https://www.igepn.edu.ec/chimborazo), and [NOAA](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/highestpoint.html) notes that its summit is the point on Earth farthest from the planet's centre. Between them, the Quilotoa crater lake sits in its caldera like a dropped piece of sky. See [the Avenue of the Volcanoes and Cotopaxi](/posts/avenue-of-the-volcanoes-and-cotopaxi/) for how to explore it.

The Andes are also where much of Ecuador's market and craft life happens. Otavalo's market fills the town with textiles and the sound of bargaining. In Cuenca, far to the south, the blue domes of the New Cathedral rise over a colonial centre, and the city is known for toquilla straw hats, the ones the rest of the world calls Panama hats. Food here is warming and built for cold evenings: locro de papa, a thick potato soup, is the dish we'd order first. Our guide to [Cuenca and the southern Andes](/posts/cuenca-and-the-southern-andes/) goes further.

What it is like to be here: crisp mornings, strong sun at midday, a jumper by dusk, and a constant sense of being high up, with mountains at the end of every street.

## The cloud forest: the bridge to the west

Drive north-west out of Quito and the road soon starts to fall. The bare highland slopes give way to trees hung with moss and bromeliads, the air turns damp, and cloud drifts through the valleys at eye level. This is the cloud forest, and Mindo is the best-known place to stay in it.

It is a world of its own in all but name. Days here are quieter and greener: birdwatching at first light, walks to waterfalls, and long spells on a lodge veranda watching hummingbirds whirr in and out of the feeders. If you only have a few days on the mainland, the cloud forest is the easiest way to feel the change from mountains to lowland without going far.

The cloud forest is the natural bridge between the Andes and the coast, and in a calmer period the road would carry on down to the Pacific. For now we'd treat Mindo as the end of that road rather than a waypoint (see the coast section below).

## The Amazon: rivers, canoes and the sound of the forest

East of the Andes the land drops into the Amazon, and the most memorable way to arrive is overland through Baños. The town sits in a deep valley under steep green slopes, a base for hot springs, cycling and waterfall walks. From there the road follows the Pastaza down past the Pailón del Diablo, the gorge narrows and steams, and the forest closes in. It is the clearest gateway from the Andes to the Amazon that we know.

Once you are in the forest, the scale of the place takes over. Ecuador Travel describes [Yasuní National Park](https://ecuador.travel/amazonia/) as the largest protected area in mainland Ecuador, home to more than 1,400 animal species and including Waorani territory. UNESCO designated the wider [Yasuní Biosphere Reserve](https://www.unesco.org/en/mab/yasuni) in 1989 and describes it as one of the most biodiverse places on Earth for its area. To the north, Ecuador Travel says the Cuyabeno reserve's lagoons are places to look for river dolphins, manatees and caimans.

What it is like to be here: you wake before dawn to a chorus of birds and insects, travel by motorised canoe, walk trails with a guide who points out what you'd never spot alone, and fall asleep to rain on a thatched roof. Our [Amazon lodge guide](/posts/staying-in-an-amazon-lodge-in-ecuador/) explains how a stay works.

Two practical notes. The [CDC](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/ecuador) recommends yellow fever vaccination for travellers going to areas below 2,300 m east of the Andes, which covers the Amazon provinces, and preventive malaria medication for Orellana, Pastaza and Sucumbíos, among others. And for Cuyabeno, the UK government's [regional risks page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/regional-risks) says to take local advice, travel in daylight with a reputable official guide and have an emergency plan; it also notes that Lago Agrio (Nueva Loja) lies inside the 20 km Colombian border zone where FCDO advises against all but essential travel.

## The coast: a world on pause for most visitors

The coast is the world we most want to write about warmly, and the one where we have to be most careful. It is the land of Guayaquil, Ecuador's big port city on the river, of Pacific beaches and fishing towns, of dry forest in the west and of a seafood kitchen built around ceviche and the restorative fish soup encebollado.

But here is the honest position today. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office [advises against all but essential travel](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/warnings-and-insurance) to the coastal provinces of Esmeraldas, Manabí, Santa Elena, Guayas, El Oro, Los Ríos and Santo Domingo de los Tsáchilas. The exception is airside transit within Guayaquil airport. Travelling against FCDO advice can invalidate your travel insurance. FCDO's [safety page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/safety-and-security) also describes a very high, mainly gang-related murder rate in Guayaquil.

On top of that, FCDO reports a 60-day state of emergency for security reasons declared from 15 September in eight provinces, including Guayas and Pichincha, the province around Quito. Check whether it has been extended before you travel.

So for now, the coast is a world most visitors see only through the window at Guayaquil airport on the way to the islands. We are not recommending beach stays, and we'd ask any operator who suggests one to explain why. Advice changes, in both directions, so check the FCDO page and your own government's advice when you plan. We hope to write a fuller coast guide when it can be visited without that caveat.

## The Galápagos: the world apart

The fourth world is the one that needs a flight. The islands lie about 1,000 km off the coast and, according to [Ecuador Travel](https://ecuador.travel/galapagos/), are reached only by air from Quito or Guayaquil, landing at Baltra (Seymour) or San Cristóbal. The flight from Guayaquil takes about 1 hour 50 minutes. FCDO advises checking with your airline that you stay airside if you connect there.

Nothing quite prepares you for the first hour. Marine iguanas lie in heaps on black lava, sea lions treat the harbour benches as their own, and blue-footed boobies go about their business beside the trail as if you weren't there. Under water it is the same: turtles, rays and, if you are lucky, penguins on the western islands. The animals are not tame so much as unafraid, and the park rules exist to keep it that way.

You can see the islands from a boat or from a hotel base. Ecuador Travel says overnight cruises run from 3 to 14 nights, with certified naturalist guides; we compare the two styles in [Galápagos cruise or island-hopping?](/posts/galapagos-cruise-or-island-hopping/). The ministry describes a [warm season from December to May and a cooler, drier season from June to November](https://ecuador.travel/como-llegar-a-galapagos/), and both have their rewards. Before you go, read [Galápagos entry fees and rules, explained](/posts/galapagos-entry-fees-and-rules/) for the entry fee, transit card and biosecurity declaration. FCDO currently also reports heavy rain and flooding on Santa Cruz Island, with disruption to travel to Baltra airport, so check its [warnings page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/warnings-and-insurance) close to your dates.

## How the worlds connect

The joins are what make an Ecuador trip work. Quito is the hub: from there the cloud forest is a short drive west, the volcanoes run south, Baños leads east to the Amazon, and flights go out to the Galápagos. A first trip that links three of the four worlds might look like this:

| Leg | How you get there |
| --- | --- |
| Quito and the cloud forest | By road from Quito |
| The Avenue of the Volcanoes | By road south from Quito |
| Baños and the Amazon | By road through the Pastaza valley, or by air |
| The Galápagos | By air from Quito or Guayaquil |

Our [first-timer's guide](/posts/first-timers-guide-to-ecuador/) covers how long to allow, and the [14-day itinerary](/posts/14-day-ecuador-and-galapagos-itinerary/) shows one way to string them together.

## Planning it

When you talk to an operator such as [Encantadas Expeditions](https://encantadasexpeditions.tourground.com/), ask:

1. Which of the four worlds can we fit in comfortably for the time we have, and which should we leave for another trip?
2. Does the route enter any province covered by an FCDO warning, including any coastal leg or connection?
3. How will the Amazon transfer work, and is the lodge in an area where FCDO asks for extra precautions?
4. Can you send a written, itemised quote, including what we pay locally in the Galápagos?
5. Can you confirm current Galápagos rules and fees in writing before we pay?

Four worlds is a lot for one trip. Pick two or three, give each enough time to sink in, and keep the rest as a reason to come back.

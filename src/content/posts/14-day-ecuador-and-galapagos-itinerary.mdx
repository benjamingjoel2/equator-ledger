Two weeks is the length of trip that lets Ecuador show you its range. In a single fortnight you can wake to church bells in Quito's old town, watch a snow cone catch the first sun above the páramo, fall asleep to frogs in the rainforest, and step off a boat onto a beach where sea lions barely lift their heads. Few countries let you change worlds so quickly.

The trick is the order. Ecuador rewards a route that respects altitude, leaves slack around flights, and saves the islands for when you are rested enough to enjoy them. This is the plan we'd suggest for a first visit, with the reasoning behind each choice and the alternatives worth considering.

## The logic of the order

**Altitude first, gently.** Quito and the Andes are high, and the [FCDO's health advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/health) notes that altitude sickness is a risk in parts of Ecuador. Starting with two easy days in Quito lets your body adjust before you go higher on the Avenue of the Volcanoes.

**Rainforest in the middle.** The Amazon sits below the Andes, so it slots in naturally after the mountains. It is also a welcome rest for lungs that have been working hard.

**Galápagos last, with a buffer.** Island flights depend on weather, and connections back to the mainland are limited. Ending with the Galápagos means a delay costs you a day of holiday, not a missed boat. We'd always fly back to the mainland at least a night before your international flight home.

## Days 1–2: Quito

Arrive, sleep, and take it slowly. Spend the first day walking the colonial centre, around the Plaza Grande and the steep streets towards La Ronda, stopping often. Eat lightly, drink plenty of water, and go easy on alcohol for the first night or two.

On day two, if you feel well, ride the TelefériQo up the slopes of Pichincha for a huge view across the city, bearing in mind that the [FCDO](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/safety-and-security) puts the top station at 4,050 m, a real step up from the city. Our [Quito in three days](/posts/quito-in-three-days/) guide has more ideas for these first days, including where to wander and what to eat.

A safety note: the FCDO reported a [60-day state of emergency from 15 September](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/warnings-and-insurance) in eight provinces, including Pichincha, where Quito is. Check whether it has been extended, and read your own government's advice before you go.

## Days 3–5: the Avenue of the Volcanoes

Head south from Quito with a driver-guide.

- **Day 3: Cotopaxi National Park.** Walk on the páramo beneath the glaciated cone, then sleep in a hacienda nearby, with an open fire and a hearty dinner.
- **Day 4: Quilotoa.** Drive west to the crater lake and walk the rim, or down to the water if you have the legs for the climb back.
- **Day 5: Baños or back to Quito.** Either drop east to Baños and the Pailón del Diablo waterfall, or return north to Quito ready for the Amazon.

Our full guide to [the Avenue of the Volcanoes and Cotopaxi](/posts/avenue-of-the-volcanoes-and-cotopaxi/) covers each stop, the climbing rules, and the option of adding Chimborazo if you have an extra day.

## Days 6–8: an Amazon lodge

Three nights in the rainforest. Depending on the lodge, you either fly from Quito to a small Amazon town or travel by road (possibly from Baños, if you ended day 5 there), then continue by canoe. Days are spent on dawn canoe trips, forest walks and night outings, with rain, heat and a constant soundtrack of insects and birds.

Health matters here. The [CDC](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/ecuador) recommends yellow fever vaccination for travellers aged nine months or older going to areas below 2,300 m east of the Andes in several provinces, including those of the Amazon, and preventive malaria medication for several of them, so see a travel health clinic well before you go. If you are considering Cuyabeno, read the [FCDO's advice on the Colombian border area](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/regional-risks) first. Our guide to [staying in an Amazon lodge](/posts/staying-in-an-amazon-lodge-in-ecuador/) covers all of this, plus what to pack.

On **day 8**, travel back to Quito and spend the night near the airport, ready for an early flight.

## Days 9–13: the Galápagos

Fly from Quito to the islands. According to Ecuador's tourism ministry, flights from the mainland go to [Seymour airport on Baltra or to San Cristóbal](https://ecuador.travel/galapagos/), and take up to about 2 h 30 from Quito with a stop in Guayaquil. The islands lie about 1,000 km off the coast.

You then have a choice: a cruise or a land base. Our full comparison is in [Galápagos cruise or island-hopping?](/posts/galapagos-cruise-or-island-hopping/), but in brief:

| | Cruise | Land base |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it works | You sleep aboard and visit sites with a naturalist guide | You stay in town and take day trips |
| Best for | Remote islands, wildlife focus | Flexibility, seasickness-prone travellers |
| On a 5-day window | A short cruise | Santa Cruz, then Isabela |

The tourism ministry says overnight cruises run [from 3 to 14 nights](https://ecuador.travel/galapagos/), so a short cruise fits this window. For a land-based version, we'd spend two or three nights on Santa Cruz, around Puerto Ayora, the Charles Darwin Research Station, Tortuga Bay and the giant tortoises of the highlands, then two nights on Isabela, with its lava landscapes, Las Tintoreras and the Sierra Negra volcano.

A current note: the FCDO reports [heavy rain and flooding on Santa Cruz Island](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/warnings-and-insurance), with an emergency declared by Gobierno Galápagos on 31 October 2026, road closures, power cuts and disruption to travel to Baltra airport. Check the latest before you travel and ask your operator about contingency plans.

On **day 13**, fly back to the mainland and spend the night in Quito.

## Galápagos logistics in brief

Our post on [Galápagos entry fees and rules](/posts/galapagos-entry-fees-and-rules/) explains everything in detail. The essentials:

- **Entry fee.** The national park's conservation fee for foreign tourists over 12 is [US$200](https://galapagos.gob.ec/tributo-de-ingreso/), collected by park rangers at Baltra and San Cristóbal airports. The tourism ministry says to pay it [in cash](https://ecuador.travel/galapagos/).
- **Transit Control Card.** The [TCT costs US$20](https://www.gobiernogalapagos.gob.ec/preguntas-y-respuestas-frecuentes-control-migratorio-tct/), after online pre-registration, and you buy it at Quito or Guayaquil airport before check-in. The Galápagos government advises arriving at the airport three hours before your flight. Tourists can spend up to 60 days a year in the islands.
- **Biosecurity.** Since 31 January 2026 the sworn goods declaration is [digital only](https://abgalapagos.gob.ec/galapagos-retira-el-formulario-fisico-de-declaracion-juramentada-de-mercancias/), and you can complete it up to 72 hours before you travel. Don't pack fresh fruit, seeds, plants or soil.
- **Insurance.** The FCDO says travel health insurance is [mandatory for foreign tourists](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/entry-requirements) in the Galápagos, and you'll also need evidence of accommodation and a return flight. Medical facilities in the islands are limited, so the [FCDO](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/health) advises making sure your insurance covers air ambulance evacuation.
- **Visitor rules.** The park's [2024 rules](https://galapagos.gob.ec/download/11826) require a park-authorised guide in protected areas, a distance of at least 2 metres from wildlife, no touching or feeding animals, and no flash photography. The park updated its rules in 2026: the resolution was published in the Registro Oficial on 14 September 2026, and press reports from [Lexis](https://www.lexis.com.ec/noticias/registro-oficial-del-dia-nuevas-reglas-de-visita-para-areas-protegidas-de-galapagos), [Vistazo](https://www.vistazo.com/sostenibilidad/2026-09-21-parque-nacional-galapagos-actualiza-reglas-visita-estas-son-nuevas-normas-restricciones-OB11238475) and [Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/sociedad/parque-nacional-galapagos-visitas-nuevas-reglas-turistas-nacionales-extranjeros-133113/) describe new minimum distances and advance reservations at some busy sites. Ask your operator for the current rules in writing.

If your flights route through Guayaquil, the FCDO advises checking with your airline that you will [stay airside](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/entry-requirements). It advises against all but essential travel to Guayas province and the other coastal provinces, except airside transit at Guayaquil airport.

## Day 14: fly home

Fly out of Quito. Having spent the night on the mainland, you'll arrive at the airport rested, with any island flight delays already absorbed.

## Alternatives and swaps

**Swap the Amazon for Cuenca.** If the rainforest's health requirements or travel days don't suit you, spend days 6–8 in the southern Andes instead. Cuenca's colonial centre, its blue cathedral domes and nearby Cajas National Park and Ingapirca make a rich few days. See [Cuenca and the southern Andes](/posts/cuenca-and-the-southern-andes/).

**Swap the Amazon for the cloud forest.** The cloud forest on the western slopes of the Andes, around Mindo, is an easy trip from Quito: misty forest, waterfalls and hummingbirds darting around the feeders. It suits shorter trips and families.

**Add the coast?** We don't suggest it for now. The FCDO currently [advises against all but essential travel](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/warnings-and-insurance) to the coastal provinces, including Esmeraldas, Manabí, Santa Elena and Guayas, and travel insurance can be invalidated if you go against that advice. Check the current advice from your own government too. Our post on [Ecuador's four worlds](/posts/ecuadors-four-worlds/) explains how the coast fits into the bigger picture.

**Longer in the islands.** If wildlife is your priority, take time from the mainland and give the Galápagos a week. Our guide to [the best time to visit the Galápagos](/posts/best-time-to-visit-the-galapagos/) can help you choose dates.

## What to confirm with your operator

Before you book, ask for:

- A day-by-day plan with every transfer, flight and night's accommodation, in writing
- A written, itemised quote, including what is and isn't included (park fees, TCT, domestic flights, guiding, meals)
- Confirmation of the current Galápagos fees and visitor rules, and any site reservations your itinerary needs
- The buffer day before your international flight, and what happens if an island flight is delayed
- How the route builds up altitude gradually, and where you sleep each night
- Current safety advice for each region, and contingency plans for weather, flooding or road closures

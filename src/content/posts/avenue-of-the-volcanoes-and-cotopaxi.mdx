Leave Quito heading south on a clear morning and the city thins out into fields, eucalyptus windbreaks and long views. Then, if the cloud is kind, Cotopaxi appears: a near-perfect white cone sitting above the valley as if someone had placed it there for effect. It is the image most people carry home from mainland Ecuador, and it is only the first of many.

Ecuador's tourism ministry describes [more than 70 volcanoes and mountains](https://ecuador.travel/avenida-de-los-volcanes-de-ecuador/) lining what it calls the Avenue of the Volcanoes, the high corridor between two ranges of the Andes. You don't need to climb any of them to enjoy the route. Most of the pleasure is in the travelling: market towns, cold bright mornings, páramo grassland the colour of lions, and a different snow peak to squint at every day. This is our guide to the road, the main stops, and how to pace it so the altitude works with you rather than against you.

## The shape of the route

Think of the Avenue as a long valley running south from Quito, with the Pan-American Highway down its spine and volcanoes on both sides. The classic stops, in order heading south, are Cotopaxi National Park, the Quilotoa crater lake (a detour to the west), the town of Baños (a detour east, where the Andes start dropping towards the Amazon), and Chimborazo, the giant of the southern end.

You can do a short version in two or three days from Quito, or stretch it to a week with a night or two at each stop. We think three nights is the minimum that feels like travel rather than transit, and the version we'd choose has four or five. If you're combining it with the islands and the rainforest, our [14-day Ecuador and Galápagos itinerary](/posts/14-day-ecuador-and-galapagos-itinerary/) gives the Avenue three days and explains why it comes early in the trip.

## Cotopaxi

Cotopaxi rises to [5,897 m, according to Ecuador's Instituto Geofísico (IG-EPN)](https://www.igepn.edu.ec/cotopaxi), about 45 km south-east of Quito. It is classed as active, and its most recent eruption ran from October 2022 to July 2023. In its latest note, dated 21 October 2026, the institute reported [low internal activity and low surface activity, both unchanged](https://www.igepn.edu.ec/servicios/noticias). Conditions on a volcano can change, so check the IG-EPN news page shortly before you go, and expect your guide to do the same.

The national park around it is wide, windswept and high. Most visitors drive in, walk on the páramo, perhaps circle a lagoon below the cone, and climb a little towards the refuge on the volcano's flank if conditions and park rules allow. Even that short walk feels like effort at this height: you take ten steps, stop, breathe, and look up at the glacier. Wild horses graze the plains, and the light changes by the minute.

### Climbing it

The UK government's travel advice says that [since November 2023 high mountaineering has been possible again in Cotopaxi National Park](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/safety-and-security), subject to park rules, and that it is illegal to do high mountaineering or climb a glaciated mountain without an official accredited guide. That applies across Ecuador, not just on Cotopaxi. A summit attempt is a serious undertaking that needs proper acclimatisation beforehand, a full kit and a guide whose accreditation you have seen. Ask your operator to confirm the current rules and the park's opening status in writing.

## Quilotoa

West of the highway, a long climb through farmland and small villages brings you to the rim of Quilotoa, a volcanic caldera holding a lake of startling green-blue. IG-EPN gives the volcano's height as [3,914 m](https://www.igepn.edu.ec/quilotoa), describes it as potentially active, and dates its last eruption to the 12th century.

The view from the rim is the reason to come, and it's worth staying the night nearby to see it in early light, before the cloud builds. A trail drops steeply to the water. Going down is easy; coming back up at this altitude is slow work, and there is no shame in taking it in small stages. Hikers with more time can walk between villages on the trails around the crater, which is one of the most rewarding ways to see rural highland life.

## Baños and the Pailón del Diablo

Baños sits on the eastern side of the Andes, where the mountains begin to fall towards the rainforest. It is warmer and greener than the high valley, with a lively small-town feel, thermal baths, and a road down the Pastaza gorge lined with waterfalls. The best known is the Pailón del Diablo, the Devil's Cauldron, a thundering cascade you reach on foot through the forest. Walkways and stairs bring you close enough to feel the spray, so bring a light waterproof.

Baños is a natural break in the itinerary. After several nights in cold, high country, a day of lower, softer air does wonders. Because the town looks east, keep health advice in mind if you continue downhill: the [CDC recommends yellow fever vaccination](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/ecuador) for travellers aged nine months or older going to areas below 2,300 m east of the Andes in several provinces, Tungurahua among them. If your trip carries on into the Amazon, read our guide to [staying in an Amazon lodge](/posts/staying-in-an-amazon-lodge-in-ecuador/) and talk to a travel health clinic well before you fly.

## Chimborazo

At the southern end of the Avenue stands Chimborazo, which IG-EPN measures at [6,263 m](https://www.igepn.edu.ec/chimborazo) and describes as the highest volcano in the northern Andes, about 150 km south of Quito. It also holds a remarkable title: because the Earth bulges at the equator, [NOAA says Chimborazo's summit is the point on Earth's surface farthest from the planet's centre](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/highestpoint.html), more than 2,072 m farther out than the top of Everest.

Seen from the road, Chimborazo is broad and heavy rather than elegant, a great white shoulder that seems to fill half the sky. Vicuñas graze the sandy plains around its base. Visitors usually drive partway up and walk a little higher if conditions allow, which at this height is enough to leave you breathless in every sense.

Climbing is another matter. The FCDO says the Chimborazo Reserve is [open only to tour operators and accredited clubs](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/safety-and-security) following a 2021 avalanche, and that access must be requested by email at least five days ahead. If a summit is your goal, plan it with an operator that can make that arrangement and supply an accredited guide.

## Altitude and acclimatisation

The [FCDO health advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/health) is plain: altitude sickness is a risk in parts of Ecuador, and so is strong UV radiation. Almost everywhere on this route is high. For a sense of scale, the FCDO notes that the top station of Quito's cable car, the TelefériQo, sits at [4,050 m](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/safety-and-security), and Cotopaxi's park and Quilotoa's rim are high country too.

What helps:

- **Start in Quito and go slowly.** Spend at least two nights in the capital before heading higher. Our [Quito in three days](/posts/quito-in-three-days/) guide is built around easy first days.
- **Sleep lower than you play.** Visit the highest viewpoints by day and sleep somewhat lower where you can.
- **Drink water, go easy on alcohol, and eat lightly** for the first couple of days.
- **Listen to your body.** Headache, nausea and poor sleep are common early signs. If symptoms get worse, go down and seek medical advice. The emergency number in Ecuador is 911, according to the [FCDO](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/health).
- **Respect the sun.** Use high-factor sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses, even when it feels cold.

Talk to your doctor before travel if you have heart or lung conditions, or if you have had altitude illness before.

## Staying in haciendas

Some of the most memorable nights on the Avenue are spent in old haciendas: country estates, some of them centuries old, now run as guesthouses. Expect thick walls, courtyards, open fires in the evening, and hearty highland cooking such as locro de papa, a potato soup that tastes even better after a cold day on the páramo. Many offer horse riding, walks across their land, or simply a quiet garden with a volcano in view.

Haciendas vary widely in style, comfort and price, and we don't name or rate specific properties. Ask your operator what is included (meals, heating, activities) and get the cost on a written, itemised quote.

## Pacing it

A route we like, starting after two nights in Quito:

| Day | Where |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Cotopaxi National Park, night in a hacienda nearby |
| 2 | Quilotoa, night near the rim |
| 3 | Baños and the Pailón del Diablo |
| 4 | Chimborazo by day, then onwards |

Add a day anywhere you feel rushed, especially at Quilotoa, where the morning view is the point. Leave room for weather, too: the volcanoes hide in cloud for days at a time, and a flexible plan gives you a second chance at the view. The [FCDO also notes](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/safety-and-security) that Ecuador's rainy season usually runs from October to May, and that the authorities have issued a red alert for a forecast 2026–2027 El Niño, so roads can be affected by landslides.

A note on security: the FCDO reported a [60-day state of emergency from 15 September](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/warnings-and-insurance) in eight provinces, including Pichincha, where Quito is. Check whether it has been extended before you travel, and read the current advice from your own government as well.

## Before you book

Questions worth putting to any operator planning this route:

- Is Cotopaxi National Park currently open, and which areas can we visit?
- If we want to climb, who is the accredited guide, and how are summit days planned around acclimatisation?
- For Chimborazo, who arranges reserve access, and how far ahead?
- Where do we sleep each night, roughly how high is it, and how does the route build up gradually?
- What happens if a road is closed or the weather turns?
- What is included in each night's stay, and can we have an itemised quote in writing?

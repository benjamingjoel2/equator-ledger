Getting to the Galápagos involves more paperwork than most short flights. There is a card to buy before you check in, a declaration to fill in on your phone, a bag check when you land, a fee to pay in cash to a park ranger, and a set of rules that follow you onto every trail and beach. None of it is difficult. It is just unfamiliar, and it all happens on a travel day when you would rather be looking out of the window for your first glimpse of the islands.

So here it is in the order you meet it, from the mainland airport to the moment you step off the plane into the equatorial glare. **We checked every figure and rule on this page against official sources on 11 November 2026, and re-checked the UK government advice on 13 November 2026.** Fees and rules do change, so confirm them with your operator in writing before you travel.

## Before you leave home

Start with the entry requirements for the islands themselves, which go beyond those for mainland Ecuador. For British passport holders, the UK government says your [passport must be valid for at least 6 months after the date you arrive](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/entry-requirements) in Ecuador, and you can stay up to 90 days without a visa. If you hold another passport, check your own government's advice.

For the Galápagos specifically, the [UK government's entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/entry-requirements) list:

- **Evidence of a hotel booking or an invitation letter.** On a cruise, your booking confirmation is what you will be asked for; check with your operator what they send.
- **Evidence of a return flight to the mainland.**
- **Travel health insurance**, which it describes as mandatory for foreign tourists.
- **A Transit Control Card**, bought at the departure airport before check-in (see below).
- **A maximum stay of 60 days.**

Insurance deserves a second look. The UK government notes there are [limited medical facilities in the Galápagos](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/health) and advises making sure your policy covers air ambulance evacuation. It also warns that [travel insurance can be invalidated](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/warnings-and-insurance) if you travel against its advice, which is relevant if you are thinking of spending time on Ecuador's coast.

## Step 1: the Transit Control Card at the mainland airport

Every flight to the islands leaves from Quito or Guayaquil, and your first stop at either airport is the Transit Control Card counter, not check-in.

The Transit Control Card (Tarjeta de Control de Tránsito, or TCT) is issued by the Galápagos government council, CGREG. According to [its official FAQ](https://www.gobiernogalapagos.gob.ec/preguntas-y-respuestas-frecuentes-control-migratorio-tct/):

- It is **compulsory for everyone visiting as a tourist** or transit passenger, and you must show it both when you enter and when you leave the province.
- It costs **US$20, paid for each trip.**
- You get it at **Quito (Mariscal Sucre) or Guayaquil (José Joaquín de Olmedo) airport before your flight**, after an online pre-registration. Advance purchase applies only to travel agencies.
- CGREG advises arriving at the airport **3 hours before your flight**.

You pre-register through the [CGREG online system](https://siig-registro.gobiernogalapagos.gob.ec/tct/emitir), which asks for your ID and your outbound and return flight details. If an operator has booked your trip, ask whether they are registering you or whether you need to do it yourself. Keep the card safe: you will need it again on the way out. The tourism ministry gives the [same US$20 figure](https://ecuador.travel/como-llegar-a-galapagos/).

If you are flying via Guayaquil, the UK government advises against all but essential travel to Guayas province except airside transit, and says to [check with your airline that you will stay airside](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/entry-requirements).

## Step 2: the biosecurity declaration and bag check

The islands' biosecurity agency, ABG, exists to stop new species arriving with visitors. Its [instructions for entering Galápagos](https://abgalapagos.gob.ec/normas-de-bioseguridad-para-ingresar-a-galapagos-biosecurity-standards-to-enter-the-galapagos-islands/) are to check the list of permitted products, let inspectors check your luggage at the destination airport, and complete a sworn goods declaration.

Since 31 January 2026, the [paper declaration form is no longer handed out](https://abgalapagos.gob.ec/galapagos-retira-el-formulario-fisico-de-declaracion-juramentada-de-mercancias/). The declaration is now digital, through ABG's online system, for all passengers over 18, and you complete it within 72 hours before travel. You then show the QR code or registration number to inspectors at Baltra or San Cristóbal airport.

What not to pack: ABG's [product list](https://abgalapagos.gob.ec/lista-de-productos/) sorts items into permitted, restricted and not permitted. The not-permitted examples include fresh flowers, live plants, soil and sand, fresh fruit, coffee beans, live animals, fresh meat, fresh cheese and fresh or pasteurised milk. The simplest rule: eat your fruit before you board. Since soil and sand are on the not-permitted list, we would also brush the dirt off your walking boots before you pack them.

## Step 3: the park entry fee at the island airport

You land at Seymour airport on Baltra or at San Cristóbal airport, and before you leave the terminal, [park rangers collect the national park entry fee](https://galapagos.gob.ec/tributo-de-ingreso/). The tourism ministry says this is [paid in cash](https://ecuador.travel/galapagos/) at the island airports, so carry it in US dollars, ideally in clean notes. Some operators include the fee in their price; ask whether yours does.

The fee is set by Ordenanza Provincial No. 001-CGREG-30-06-2025. These are the amounts shown on the [park's fee page](https://galapagos.gob.ec/tributo-de-ingreso/) and in its [official fee table](https://galapagos.gob.ec/2025/files/tasa_esp_ing.pdf):

| Visitor category | Over 12 | Under 12 |
|---|---|---|
| Foreign tourist | US$200 | US$100 |
| CAN or Mercosur national, not resident in Ecuador | US$100 | US$50 |
| Ecuadorian national or foreign resident of Ecuador | US$30 | US$15 |

A few more details from the same page:

- Ecuadorian nationals and residents over 65, or holding a CONADIS disability card, pay US$15.
- Non-resident foreign students enrolled at Ecuadorian educational institutions pay US$50.
- Children under 2, of any nationality, are exempt.
- CAN countries are listed as Bolivia, Colombia, Ecuador and Peru; Mercosur as Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay and Uruguay.

The park's page does not state an effective date for these amounts, so if you have seen a different figure elsewhere, ask your operator to confirm the current one.

## Where the fee money goes

It helps to know the fee is not just a ticket. A chart on the [park's fee page](https://galapagos.gob.ec/tributo-de-ingreso/) shows how the money is shared:

- **45%** to the Galápagos National Park
- **25%** to municipal governments
- **20%** to the Galápagos government council (CGREG)
- **5%** to the biosecurity agency (ABG)
- **5%** to parish governments

In other words, the fee pays for the rangers on the trails, the inspectors at the airport and the local councils of the islands where people live.

## Step 4: the rules on the islands

Once you are through the airport, the park's visitor rules apply every time you set foot in a protected area. The official rules on the park's website are from 2024, set out in [Resolución 0000007](https://galapagos.gob.ec/download/11826) and summarised on its [visitor rules page](https://galapagos.gob.ec/reglas-de-visita/). The main points:

- Visit protected sites only with a **guide authorised by the park**, and use authorised operators and boats.
- Stay on trails and follow the signs.
- Keep **at least 2 metres from animals**, including your camera.
- Do not touch or feed animals. No flash photography.
- **Drones need park authorisation.**
- Take nothing away: no sand, coral, shells, volcanic rock or animal parts.
- Carry out your rubbish. No graffiti, alcohol, smoking or campfires in the protected area.
- No speakers; use headphones.
- **Visiting hours are 06:00 to 18:00.**
- Sunscreens containing oxybenzone or octinoxate are restricted, so check the label.

**The 2026 update.** The park updated its visitor rules in 2026. Press reports from [Lexis](https://www.lexis.com.ec/noticias/registro-oficial-del-dia-nuevas-reglas-de-visita-para-areas-protegidas-de-galapagos), [Vistazo](https://www.vistazo.com/sostenibilidad/2026-09-21-parque-nacional-galapagos-actualiza-reglas-visita-estas-son-nuevas-normas-restricciones-OB11238475) and [Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/sociedad/parque-nacional-galapagos-visitas-nuevas-reglas-turistas-nacionales-extranjeros-133113/) say the new resolution was published in the Registro Oficial on 14 September 2026. They describe new minimum distances, including for kayaks and pangas and from whales and dolphins, and advance reservations at some busy sites, including the tortoise centres, Las Grietas and Volcán Chico. We could not read the official text ourselves, so treat those details as reported, and ask your operator for the current rules in writing.

## Step 5: leaving the islands

On the way out, keep your Transit Control Card to hand, because you show it again when leaving the province. The [CGREG FAQ](https://www.gobiernogalapagos.gob.ec/preguntas-y-respuestas-frecuentes-control-migratorio-tct/) says tourists may stay up to 60 days a year in total, counted from the first entry, with no extension, which matters if you are planning a long stay or more than one visit in a year.

One current note: the UK government reports [heavy rain and flooding on Santa Cruz Island](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/warnings-and-insurance), with an emergency declared by Gobierno Galápagos on 31 October 2026 and disruption to travel to Baltra airport. Allow extra time and check the latest before you fly.

## Before you book

Questions worth putting to your operator, in writing:

- Is the park entry fee included in my price, or do I pay it in cash on arrival?
- Will you pre-register my Transit Control Card, or should I?
- Does your booking confirmation count as proof of accommodation for the islands?
- Which visitor sites on my itinerary need advance reservations under the 2026 rules, and have you made them?
- Does my insurance meet the islands' requirements, including air ambulance evacuation?
